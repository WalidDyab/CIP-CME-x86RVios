Curriculum Intelligence Portal

About the Portal

A central interface supporting Electrical Engineering curricula information, assessment, review, accreditation-related documentation, and continuous improvement.

Purpose

The portal makes curriculum and assessment information easier to access, review, maintain, analyze, communicate, and document for faculty and committees. It supports continuous-improvement and accreditation-related activities; formal curriculum decisions remain subject to the appropriate faculty, committee, and institutional approval processes.

  • Teaching strategies and assessment methods

Review and Reporting

One Common Governance Path

Both semester review windows follow the same academic process.

  1. Faculty Proposal

    Faculty submit corrections, updates, proposed modifications, and improvement suggestions during the announced window.

  2. Curriculum Committee Review & Consolidation

    The Committee reviews rationale, combines related proposals, checks consistency and prerequisites, considers CLO/SO/PI and assessment effects where relevant, seeks clarification when needed, and prepares recommendations.

  3. Department Council Approval

    Recommended modifications proceed through the established academic approval process.

  4. Canonical Curriculum Update

    Only approved changes are incorporated into official curriculum information.

  5. QA

    Updated data, mappings, pages, reports, and affected relationships are checked before release.

  6. CIP Release for the Following Semester

    The approved release is made available before or around the following semester so faculty can prepare syllabi, teaching materials, assessments, and course documentation.

A faculty submission is a proposal, not an approved curriculum change. Only changes that complete academic approval enter canonical curriculum information. A CIP release represents an approved curriculum state.

Frequently Asked Questions

BSc EE curriculum questions. These answers and the governance section above apply to the undergraduate Electrical Engineering curriculum; Purpose and Key Features describe the shared portal.

Understanding BSc EE Information

Which BSc EE information is approved curriculum data, and which is instructor guidance?

Released course information, approved course learning outcomes (CLOs), Student Outcome (SO) and Performance Indicator (PI) mappings, assessment-plan information, and current textbooks and references are approved records. Standards & Codes course relevance and priority, Stakeholders & Design Context suggestions, teaching examples, and assessment prompts are instructor guidance. Guidance does not change approved curriculum mappings.

How do CLOs, SOs, and PIs relate to one another?

A CLO states what students should learn in a course. An SO describes a broader capability expected of graduates, and a PI identifies a more specific aspect of that SO. Approved course mappings connect CLOs to the SOs and PIs they support.

What is the difference between curriculum mappings and the program assessment plan?

Mappings show where CLOs support SOs and PIs across the curriculum. The assessment plan identifies when outcomes are formally assessed and which courses provide evidence for program evaluation. A mapped course is not automatically assessed in every semester.

What do Introduced, Practiced, and Mastered mean in the program heatmaps?

I, P, and M show the intended progression of a PI across courses: students first encounter it, then develop it through practice, then demonstrate it at a more advanced level. The labels describe curriculum progression, not an attainment score.

Reviewing Courses and Outcomes

Does a mapped CLO in the SO Leader Dashboard mean that the outcome has been assessed?

No. A mapping shows how a course contributes to an outcome. Formal assessment follows the program assessment plan and requires actual student evidence; mapping alone does not establish attainment.

Where can I check the approved teaching and assessment methods for my course?

Use the Course Dashboard to review the methods recorded for a selected course. Teaching & Assessment shows the approved general pools and the course-specific exceptions for EE 490 and EE 492, alongside professional ethics guidance. Teaching examples are resources to use selectively, not additional approved course requirements.

What should faculty do if curriculum information appears incorrect?

Note the course and item, document the discrepancy, and submit it through the established Curriculum Committee review channel. Include it in the faculty submission during the modification cycle; urgent factual errors can be reported to the Committee outside that cycle for review.

Proposals and Curriculum Changes

Does generating a proposed CLO or reference change modify the approved curriculum?

No. A generated proposal or review report documents a suggested change. It does not approve the change or update approved curriculum records.

What happens after I generate a CLO review report?

The report records the proposed change for review by relevant faculty and the Curriculum Committee. It remains a proposal; an accepted change enters a future approved release only after the required academic review and approval.

How are textbook or reference changes proposed?

In the Course Dashboard, compare current approved entries with proposed book entries, select the changes, and provide a justification. Generate a change-request PDF for Curriculum Committee review. The request itself does not approve or apply the changes.

What can I print or export from the BSc EE portal?

Current-information outputs include course reports and the program-wide textbook and reference list. Stakeholders & Design Context can print or save a teaching guide or customized analysis. CLO, SO-mapping, and reference review tools generate proposal reports. Printing or exporting does not change approved data.

Review Windows & Approved Releases

When can faculty propose curriculum modifications?

CIP normally has two BSc EE review windows: around mid-October in the Fall semester and around mid-March in the Spring semester. Each call normally remains open for approximately two weeks; the Curriculum Committee announces the exact deadline.

What happens after I submit a proposed curriculum modification?

The Curriculum Committee reviews the rationale, consolidates related proposals, checks curriculum dependencies and relevant mapping or assessment effects, and may ask for clarification. It then recommends changes for Department Council consideration, following the governance path above.

When does a curriculum modification become official in CIP?

A submission or Committee recommendation remains a proposal. It becomes official in CIP only after academic approval, incorporation into canonical curriculum information, QA, and inclusion in a new approved release.

Which CIP release should I use when preparing my syllabus and course materials?

Use the approved release prepared for the semester you are about to teach. The Fall review cycle feeds the following Spring release; the Spring review cycle feeds the following Fall release.

Standards & Professional Practice

Does a standard listed for my course have to be taught in full?

No. Standards & Codes provides curated professional references for teaching. Core, Useful, and Advanced describe suggested teaching relevance, not a new curriculum requirement. Use the clauses, definitions, procedures, examples, or design constraints that fit the activity.

Where can I obtain a listed standard, and what do the access labels mean?

Standards & Codes links to official sources. “Free — IEEE GET” means no-cost access through IEEE GET; “Free — ITU-R,” “Free — ETSI,” and “Free — 3GPP” point to those organizations’ official sources. “Subscription / purchase” may require institutional access or purchase. The ethics codes also have official links and packaged PDFs.

How should I use the IEEE Code of Ethics and the Saudi Council of Engineers’ Engineer’s Charter in a course activity?

Standards & Codes holds the formal code records and source links; Teaching & Assessment explains their teaching role. In a short case study, students might compare two engineering decisions, identify affected stakeholders and professional responsibilities, then justify an action using a relevant code principle and its likely consequences.

Stakeholders & Engineering Context

Are the stakeholder and design-context suggestions approved CLO, SO, or PI mappings?

No. They are prompts for instructor consideration, separate from approved curriculum mappings. Selecting or adapting them does not change the CLO, SO, or PI relationships recorded for a course.

Must I use every stakeholder and contextual factor shown for my course?

No. Choose what matters to the particular engineering problem, laboratory, assignment, project, or design activity. The suggestions help reveal possibilities; not every factor applies in every situation.

How can I use Stakeholders & Design Context to teach and assess these ideas?

The page pairs plain-language definitions with course interpretations, examples, teaching ideas, assessment prompts, and relevant standards where appropriate. Prompts such as “You may ask students to…” invite application: for example, students could compare two solutions and explain how service reliability affects the people who depend on them.

What does “Customize for this course” do?

The default view is a teaching guide. Customize opens fields to adapt stakeholders, concerns, teaching and assessment ideas, requirements, evidence, and standards for an activity. Choices stay on the page until it is reloaded; print or save as PDF to keep a copy. Customization does not change approved curriculum data.

AI Use, Data Integrity, and Human Oversight

The Curriculum Committee (CC) of the Communications and Networks Engineering Department, as the academic owner and custodian of this Curriculum Intelligence Portal, follows ABET’s policy on the responsible use of artificial intelligence in accreditation activities.

Consistent with ABET guidance, AI-assisted tools may be used in this portal to:

  1. Gather and synthesize information in preparation for accreditation review.
  2. Support the collection, analysis, evaluation, and visualization of assessment data for continuous improvement.
  3. Assist in developing and organizing supporting materials related to accreditation and curriculum activities.

AI is used as a data-processing, analysis, visualization, and decision-support tool. It is not used to fabricate evidence, invent curriculum or assessment data, alter documented results, manipulate collected data, or misrepresent information.

All authoritative curriculum data, assessment evidence, mappings, and accreditation materials remain subject to verification and approval by qualified faculty and the Curriculum Committee. AI supports human judgment and decision-making; it does not replace them.

AI use is acknowledged transparently, while responsibility for the accuracy, integrity, and academic validity of the information presented remains with the program and its faculty.

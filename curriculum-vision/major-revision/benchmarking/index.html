Scope and method

Benchmarking approach

The benchmarking exercise compares selected curriculum characteristics across Saudi and international institutions. Because institutional and national requirements differ, the results are used as directional evidence rather than as a prescriptive model.

Comparisons were developed from the available official four-year study plans, excluding preparatory-year courses. Values are retained in their original credit-hour context. Saudi and international institutions were examined through two complementary lenses:

Saudi comparison

University-level requirements—including Arabic and Islamic studies, English and communication, computing and AI literacy, professional and study skills, and student-selected breadth—alongside co-op and internship structures, technical electives, and total degree credits.

  • Alfaisal University
  • King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM)
  • Prince Mohammad Bin Fahd University (PMU)
  • King Saud University (KSU)
  • King Abdulaziz University (KAU)

Reference curriculum

PSU curriculum structure

The PSU structures provide context for where future modifications may occur. The engineering programs shown (CME, EE, CM, PM, and CEE) share a 138-credit structure: 23 university + 42 college + 63 program + 10 internship credits. The 63 program credits include technical electives and the senior design project; the co-op is a separate component.

University requirements · 23 credits

  • English and communication: ENG101 Intensive Writing (3), COM201 Communication Skills (3)
  • Psychology: PSY101 Introduction to Psychology (3)
  • Arabic: ARAB101 Arabic Writing (2), ARAB103 Professional and Literary Writing (2), ARAB203 Arabic Grammar (2)
  • Islamic studies: ISC101 Islamic Ethics (2), ISC103 Islamic Economic System (2), ISC105 Studies of the Holy Quran (2), ISC203 New Financial Transactions (2)

Engineering college core · 42 credits

  • Programming, chemistry, statistics, calculus I–II, differential equations, physics I–II
  • Technical writing and translation, engineering drawing and design
  • Ethics and social aspects of engineering; engineering economy

Electrical Engineering · 63 credits

  • Engineering mathematics, linear algebra, project management
  • Circuits, electronics, logic design, signals, machines, power electronics and power systems
  • Microprocessors, control, electromagnetics, communications, senior design project
  • Two 3-credit program electives

Internal PSU curriculum reference

  • CCIS Computer Science, Information Systems, and Software Engineering each total 134 credits: 23 university + 34 college + 67 program + 10 internship credits.
  • CS program detail: 41 core, 15 CS electives, 3 business background, 6 free electives, and 2 physical education credits. A separate 10-credit internship completes the 77-credit program-and-training subtotal.
  • AI/Data Science, Machine Learning, Digital Image Processing, and NLP are CS elective options for the AI track or with CS department-chair approval.
  • This internal reference is not part of the 13 external comparator institutions.

For Electrical Engineering, EE492 Cooperative Education provides the separate 10-credit, seven-month co-op experience. Two 3-credit program electives account for the six technical-elective credits within the program requirements.

Key findings

What the comparison shows

1 — A fully prescribed university-level block

PSU’s 23 university-level credits are assigned to named requirements, with no student-selected breadth component. Alfaisal provides nine breadth-elective credits; KFUPM and PMU each provide three. The international comparator blocks shown are student-selected.

PSU’s university-level curriculum is more prescribed, while several comparator institutions provide greater student choice. Neither model is automatically superior.

2 — A comparatively high Arabic and Islamic studies allocation

PSU assigns 14 combined credits. The Saudi comparison shows Alfaisal at 0, KFUPM, PMU, and KSU at 8 each, and KAU at 14.

PSU shares the highest combined allocation in the Saudi comparison, alongside King Abdulaziz University. This is an important structural characteristic of the curriculum.

3 — A comparatively high total degree requirement

PSU requires 138 credits, within the Saudi comparator range of 128–139 and close to its upper end. The international programs shown range from 120–129 credits.

PSU’s degree is comparable in size to the larger Saudi programs but exceeds the international programs included. This points to examining curriculum efficiency in Phase 4, rather than prescribing a credit reduction.

4 — A substantial co-op experience

PSU assigns 10 credits to a seven-month co-op. Other Saudi programs shown use shorter internships or lower-credit practical-training arrangements.

PSU’s co-op represents a distinctive and substantial component of the student experience—a curricular strength to preserve and use effectively when evaluating future modifications.

5 — Limited technical-elective capacity

PSU assigns six technical-elective credits. The Saudi comparison shows Alfaisal at 15, KFUPM at 13, PMU at 6, KSU at 30, and KAU at 6.

PSU is at the lower end of the Saudi comparison, which may limit opportunities for specialization and emerging-topic choice. This is an area for examination in Phase 4, not a final recommendation.

6 — Different approaches to flexibility

The international institutions shown allocate their compared university-level blocks to student-selected requirements rather than prescribed named PSU-style courses.

The international comparison illustrates a structurally more flexible approach to university-level breadth and student choice. Its application must reflect Saudi and PSU requirements rather than directly copying an international model.

From evidence to development

How the findings will be used

The benchmarking findings do not determine the revised curriculum. They identify areas that should be examined when proposed modifications are developed in Phase 4. Each proposed modification will then be evaluated through the Phase 5 impact analysis before being considered for approval.

  • Balance between prescribed requirements and student choice
  • Overall degree-credit efficiency
  • Technical-elective capacity
  • Opportunities for emerging technical areas
  • Preservation and effective use of the co-op experience
  • Alignment between university, college, and program requirements
